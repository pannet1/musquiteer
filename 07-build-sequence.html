Step-by-Step Linear Implementation

Follow these 9 sequential steps in exact order. There are no branches or alternative paths.

Step 1: Procure the Exact Bill of Materials

Acquire the exact items specified in 3. Budget: 65mm Whoop frame, 4x 0802 19,000KV brushless motors, 31mm 4-blade props, AIO ESP32-S3 flight controller, 2x 1S LiHV batteries (BT2.0/A30), 100μF cap, OV9281 USB camera, 850nm filter/LEDs, and base-station ESP32-S3 module.

Step 2: Mechanical Assembly & Airframe Rigging

  1. Invert frame. Install four 0802 motors using M1.4 × 3mm screws with Loctite 222.
  2. Route motor wires along underside duct struts; secure with Kapton tape.
  3. Press 31mm propellers onto shafts (Front-Left CW, Front-Right CCW, Rear-Left CCW, Rear-Right CW).
  4. Verify ≥0.8mm radial clearance between blade tips and duct inner walls.

CHECK RESISTANCE BEFORE POWER-ON: Multimeter between VBAT and GND must read > 1kΩ. Solder 100uF cap directly at pads.

Step 3: Power Distribution & Flight Electronics Wiring

  1. Solder BT2.0/A30 pigtail (red to VBAT, black to GND) on the AIO board.
  2. Solder 100μF 10V low-ESR capacitor across VBAT and GND pads.
  3. Solder motor phase wires directly to ESC pads on the corners of the board.
  4. Mount AIO board on rubber dampeners with board arrow pointing forward.
  5. Verify resistance across battery leads >1kΩ with a digital multimeter.

Step 4: Flash Firmware & Calibrate Attitude Loop

  1. Flash ESP32-S3 with ESP-IDF firmware. Configure SPI bus to ICM-42688 at 24 MHz (1 kHz sample rate).
  2. Set flight PID update rate to 500 Hz; configure motor output protocol to DShot300.
  3. Run level calibration to store zero-rate gyro offsets and accelerometer gravity vector in flash.
  4. Verify correct motor rotation directions via bench test pulses (props removed).

Step 5: Assemble Stationary NIR Optical Base Station

  1. Fit 120° lens and 850nm bandpass filter to OV9281 camera. Mount 850nm IR LED ring around lens.
  2. Mount assembly on tripod in room corner at 30° downward tilt.
  3. Connect camera to USB 3.0 port and connect base ESP32-S3 dongle to another USB port.

Step 6: Deploy Base-Station Target Tracking Software

  1. Stream monochrome frames at 1280 × 800 at 100 FPS (exposure locked to 1.5 ms).
  2. Compute temporal difference: D(t) = |I(t) - I(t-1)| to eliminate static background.
  3. Apply binary threshold T=30 and 3x3 morphological open operation to clean shot noise.
  4. Extract centroid (u, v) for clusters measuring 2 to 15 pixels.
  5. Update 4-state Kalman filter to estimate smoothed target position and heading vector.

Step 7: Configure Low-Latency Telemetry Link

  1. Pair host ESP32-S3 and drone AIO on ESP-NOW channel 6 (unencrypted mode).
  2. Transmit 12-byte CommandPacket (x, y, z, yaw, state flags, checksum) at 100 Hz.
  3. On drone Core 0, receive packet and write target vector into atomic buffer for Core 1 flight loop.

Step 8: Calibration & Tethered Interception Test

  1. Calibrate perspective matrix using four floor markers (1m × 1m square).
  2. Suspend drone via 1.5m ceiling tether through frame center-of-gravity.
  3. Inject synthetic moving IR target; confirm drone yaw-aligns within 100 ms and tracks smoothly.

Step 9: Live Flight Engagement & Mechanical Ingestion

  1. Ascend to 1.0m hover via base-station optical feedback.
  2. On mosquito detection (500 Hz wingbeat cadence + 2-10 pixel size), transition to Intercept mode.
  3. Drone accelerates along interception vector at 1.5 m/s.
  4. Ducted fan suction pulls mosquito into 28,000 RPM polycarbonate blades for instant mechanical neutralization.
  5. Drone disengages and returns to 1.0m hover center.
